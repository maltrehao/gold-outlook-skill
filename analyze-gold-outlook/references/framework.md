## 1. Core pricing identity

Treat gold as a non-sovereign monetary asset with no contractual cash flow. Its price is the result of five interacting forces rather than a single inflation hedge:

\[
\text{Gold demand premium} \approx -\text{real-rate opportunity cost} - \text{USD strength} + \text{liquidity} + \text{risk/credibility premium} + \text{official and private demand}
\]

This is a reasoning identity, not an estimable one-line valuation formula. The sign of each term can change by regime, and the market responds to surprises and rates of change more than to static levels.

The main short-to-medium anchor is the expected real return on safe dollar assets. When real yields fall, holding non-yielding gold becomes less costly; when they rise, gold normally faces valuation pressure. The dollar changes the price paid by non-dollar buyers and reflects relative U.S. growth, rates, funding demand, and safe-haven flows. In crises, gold and the dollar can rise together because both receive defensive demand.

The structural premium comes from confidence in the purchasing power and policy discipline of sovereign currencies. Persistent fiscal deficits, rising interest expense, monetization risk, sanctions-driven reserve diversification, and central-bank buying can support gold even when real yields remain positive. This is why gold is better understood as a hedge against adverse changes in real-rate and monetary-fiscal credibility than as a simple hedge against current CPI.

## 2. Causal transmission chain

Start from the shock, not the indicator. Translate each event through this sequence:

**Shock → growth/inflation expectations → Fed path and Treasury term premium → nominal and real yields → dollar and liquidity → positioning/flows → gold price.**

For example, a weak employment print can support gold if it lowers expected policy rates and real yields. The same weak print may fail to support gold if it triggers a dollar funding squeeze, forced deleveraging, or a rise in term premium due to fiscal concerns. An oil shock can support gold through inflation and geopolitical risk, but hurt it if the market responds by repricing the Fed more hawkishly and lifting real yields.

Treasury financing belongs in the transmission mechanism. Large net issuance can raise term premium and drain bank reserves when it is funded from deposits rather than money-market balances. Bill issuance may be absorbed from ON RRP with less reserve pressure, while coupon-heavy issuance can exert more duration and liquidity pressure. Do not infer the gold impact from issuance size alone.

## 3. Horizon map

| Horizon | Typical window | Dominant variables | Main question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Short | Days to 6 weeks | Real-yield change, DXY, Fed repricing, positioning, ETF/futures flows, gold-silver behavior, miner confirmation | Is the next move already crowded, and what catalyst changes marginal pricing? |
| Medium | 1 to 6 months | Fed cycle, growth/inflation mix, Treasury financing, liquidity, central-bank/ETF demand, earnings and margin expectations of miners | Is the macro regime turning in a durable direction? |
| Long | 6 months to 3+ years | Fiscal trajectory, debt-service burden, monetary-fiscal credibility, reserve diversification, mine supply and structural demand | Is the monetary premium of gold expanding or contracting? |

Do not let a short-term oscillator overturn a long-term regime call. State the views separately, then connect them through entry timing: for example, “long-term constructive, medium-term neutral, short-term vulnerable to real-yield rebound.”

## 4. Regime interpretation

### Disinflationary easing

Falling inflation with slowing growth usually lowers nominal and real yields. Gold benefits when the dollar does not rise enough to offset the rate effect. If easing is fully priced, the trade may require additional evidence from ETF flows or miner breadth.

### Reflationary easing

Policy eases while inflation expectations and fiscal impulse rise. Gold can perform strongly because real yields fall or credibility risk rises. Silver and miners often broaden the move; a falling gold-silver ratio with both metals rising is a useful confirmation.

### Hawkish inflation shock

Inflation surprises higher and the Fed path/real yields rise. This is often bearish for gold at first, even though gold is called an inflation hedge. Gold becomes more resilient only if fiscal or credibility concerns dominate the higher opportunity cost.

### Growth scare or funding stress

Gold may initially fall with other liquid assets during deleveraging, especially if the dollar spikes. A later rally becomes more likely after liquidity support, falling real yields, or a reversal in funding stress. Separate the liquidation phase from the policy-response phase.

### Fiscal dominance or credibility stress

Long yields and gold can rise together if nominal yields rise because of term premium, supply, or fiscal-risk compensation rather than stronger real growth. Check inflation breakevens, yield-curve composition, auction quality, foreign demand, and the dollar before labeling this regime.

## 5. Instrument translation

USD gold is the global monetary benchmark. RMB gold approximately combines USD gold and USD/CNY, plus local premium, taxes, market segmentation, and short-run basis effects. A China-based investor can therefore earn a positive RMB return even when USD gold is flat if the renminbi weakens.

Gold ETFs add tracking error, fees, liquidity, and—in China—possible premium/discount effects. Gold miners add operational leverage: revenue follows realized gold prices, while profit follows gold prices minus energy, labor, grades, royalties, capex, and financing costs. Miners therefore have higher upside beta but are not “leveraged gold” in every regime.

## 6. Technical overlay

Use technical analysis as a timing and risk layer on top of the causal framework. Price structure can reveal whether the market is accepting or rejecting the macro thesis before slower data confirm it, but it does not explain the durable source of value by itself. For full rules, read [technical-analysis.md](technical-analysis.md).

In the short horizon, emphasize daily and intraday trend, breakout quality, volatility, volume/open interest, and positioning. In the medium horizon, emphasize daily/weekly structure, the 20/50/200-day trend stack, major swing levels, and whether pullbacks preserve higher lows. In the long horizon, use monthly/weekly structure only as confirmation; fiscal credibility, real rates, and official demand remain the primary thesis drivers.

Treat a macro/technical alignment as higher conviction. When they diverge, do not average them mechanically. Explain whether the technical signal is a likely timing delay, a warning that the macro thesis is already priced, or evidence that the assumed causal regime is wrong.

## 7. Conflict resolution

Resolve conflicting signals in this order:

1. Identify whether the conflict is across horizons or within one horizon.
2. Determine whether indicators share the same causal source. Collapse redundant evidence into one family.
3. Prefer market prices that directly represent the causal variable—such as TIPS real yields—over narrative proxies, but check liquidity and technical distortions.
4. Distinguish level from change. High real yields with a sharp decline can be tactically bullish while remaining a structural headwind.
5. Ask which variable is surprising the market. Forecasts depend on change relative to expectations, not only on the absolute data.
6. Lower conviction when the causal chain is incomplete. Express the view as conditional and specify the confirming trigger.
7. When macro and technical evidence conflict, state which horizon each belongs to and specify the price or macro event that resolves the conflict.
