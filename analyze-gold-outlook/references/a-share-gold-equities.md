## 1. Why miners may lead

A-share gold miners can lead bullion when equity investors price future RMB gold revenue, operating margins, production growth, and earnings revisions before the move is fully visible in spot gold. Their equity duration and operating leverage can amplify a small expected change in gold margins into a larger share-price move. This is the economic basis for using miners as a forward-looking signal.

The lead is conditional. Miner shares also reflect the A-share risk premium, USD/CNY, energy and labor costs, grades and recovery rates, hedging, capex, M&A, dividends, regulation, copper or other by-product exposure, and company-specific execution. A miner rally is informative about bullion only after these alternative explanations are tested.

## 2. Basket construction

Build a transparent basket from current filings rather than a permanent name list. Include listed companies with meaningful gold revenue or profit exposure and adequate liquidity. Record each company's gold revenue share, gold production share, jurisdiction, hedge policy, by-product exposure, and major corporate events.

Maintain two versions when data permit:

- A purer gold basket weighted equally or by free-float market capitalization, with a minimum gold-exposure threshold.
- A broad precious-metals basket that includes diversified miners, used only as a robustness check.

Avoid survivorship bias: define membership as of the analysis date. State whether prices are total-return adjusted and whether weights are rebalanced daily, monthly, or quarterly.

## 3. Signal construction

Calculate the following over 5-, 20-, and 60-trading-day windows:

1. Basket return and excess return versus CSI 300 or a broad A-share index.
2. Excess return versus RMB gold, which helps isolate equity and margin expectations from bullion translation.
3. Breadth: share of constituents above their 20-day moving averages and share with positive excess return.
4. Volume confirmation: turnover relative to the 60-day median.
5. Earnings confirmation: direction and breadth of consensus EPS or profit revisions when a reliable source is available.
6. Lead-lag check: rolling cross-correlation between basket excess returns and future RMB/USD gold returns. Report instability and never optimize the lag on the same sample without out-of-sample testing.

Do not claim a stable “2–3x beta” without estimating it over a disclosed window. Estimate rolling beta to RMB gold and to the broad equity index, and show confidence intervals or at least sample size. Beta changes with margins and market regime.

## 4. Attribution checklist

Before classifying miner strength as gold-positive, answer:

- Did USD gold rise, or did RMB gold rise mainly because USD/CNY rose?
- Did energy, labor, treatment, or financing costs change enough to explain margins?
- Did the move coincide with an A-share small-cap, resource, or high-beta rally?
- Was there M&A, reserve discovery, production guidance, dividend, ownership, regulatory, or accident news?
- Are pure-play miners and diversified miners moving together?
- Is breadth broad, or is the index driven by one large constituent?
- Are earnings revisions and gold forward prices confirming the equity move?

If company or equity-market factors dominate, reduce the signal confidence rather than forcing a gold conclusion.

## 5. Interpretation matrix

| Miners vs A-shares | Miners vs RMB gold | Breadth/volume | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Strong | Strong | Broad and rising | Potential forward gold/margin signal; seek confirmation from real yields, dollar, flows, and revisions |
| Strong | Weak | Broad | Mostly bullion beta or RMB translation; limited evidence of a lead |
| Strong | Strong | Narrow | Possible company or index-concentration effect; low-confidence lead |
| Weak | Weak | Deteriorating | Bearish confirmation or margin/cost concern |
| Weak | Strong | Mixed | Bullion weak but miners resilient; investigate production, costs, corporate events, and A-share beta |

Use A-share miners as one cross-asset family in the final score. Never let multiple miner submetrics overwhelm the macro evidence; aggregate them into a single family impact first.
